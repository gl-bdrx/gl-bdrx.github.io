Hi! I am an applied microeconomist, and a PhD candidate at UC Davis. My research uses the tools of environmental, labor, and development economics to understand how individuals and communuities adapt to environmental risk and technological change. 

In my job market paper, I study how environmental shocks shape rural-urban migration decisions, and show how financial constraints can severely restrict rural communities' ability to use migration as an adaptation strategy. I also have work on the role of electrification in US rural development, price sensitivity in disaster insurance markets, and non-market valuation of water quality.

## Research in Progess
* [**Climate Migration, Liquidity Constraints: Evidence from the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend (Job Market Paper)**](https://gl-bdrx.github.io//files/Boudreaux_JMP_1021)
*with Matt Reimer* <br>
  <details class="abstract">
    <summary>Abstract</summary>
    <small>Environmental shocks can increase the incentive to migrate but decrease the liquid assets needed to move, trapping some households in place. These opposing effects can mute migration responses, especially in rural economies where liquidity constraints are likely to bind. Yet separating them empirically is difficult because environmental shocks and income are jointly determined. We overcome this challenge using administrative migration data from rural Alaska, fine-scale information on heat shocks and land degradation, and the Permanent Fund Dividend, a universal annual cash transfer whose size varies independently of local environmental conditions. We find that land degradation increases rural-urban migration, while heat shocks decrease migration when the dividend is low but increase it when the dividend is high. Dividend payments offset 83% of the heat-induced decline in migration, relative to no payments. This effect is concentrated in low-wage communities and costly long-distance moves, consistent with a risk-based poverty trap.</small>
  </details>
